    Market Maker

    You are the liquidity provider. You continuously quote a bid (you buy) and an ask (you sell). Noise traders pay your spread, which is how you make money. Informed traders know the true value and only trade with you when your quote is stale, which is how you lose it. The reference price you see lags the truth.

    Spread vs volumeA wider spread earns more per fill but gets fewer fills. A tighter spread gets more fills, and more of them come from informed traders.
    Inventory riskEvery fill moves your position. Skew your quotes to lean against it, or pay to hedge. The limit is ±25.
    Adverse selectionIf the price keeps moving against you after you trade, you were picked off. Watch the headlines and the order-flow imbalance.

    Three rounds of 60 seconds: quiet tape, news day and toxic flow. Beat the Avellaneda–Stoikov bot, which quotes optimally for inventory risk but can’t read news or flow.

    How the bot quotes, and what a markout is

    Avellaneda–Stoikov (2008). Reservation price r = s − q·γ·σ²·(T − t); total spread δ = γσ²(T − t) + (2/γ)·ln(1 + γ/k), where s is the reference price, q inventory, γ risk aversion, σ volatility and k the order-arrival decay. It leans against inventory and tightens as the session ends.

    Markout at h = side × (true value at t+h − fill price). Positive at h = 0 is spread you captured. A curve that falls as h grows means adverse selection: the flow you traded with was informed.

    P&L split. Edge at fill = Σ side × (true value − price) at the moment of each fill. Inventory = everything else: how your position moved with the market afterwards.
